# Rally's next chapter — presenter closing draft

This is proposed product direction, not a statement about Meta's acquisition plans, existing partnerships or completed integrations. Keep the working demonstration central and reserve roughly the final 25 seconds for this vision. Trim five seconds from the earlier model recap if needed; confirm timing against the actual recorded take.

## Recommended spoken close

“Today, Rally helps a team leave a meeting knowing what happens next.

Now imagine that same continuity from a WhatsApp conversation, into a meeting, and through the follow-up—with your permission, and built around Meta AI.

That's the next chapter: a shared place for people and AI to move work forward together.

Because the best thing a meeting can produce… is one less meeting.”

Delivery: start grounded in the product just demonstrated. Look into the lens for “Now imagine.” Let the final line land with a small smile and a short pause. Avoid a rushed list of products or model names. Measure the take; shorten before speeding it up.

## Visual treatment

Begin on the actual evidence-linked report, with a confirmed next action visible. Transition into a clearly labeled “Future direction” graphic: Conversation → Meeting → Follow-through. Add “Proposed WhatsApp connection” beside the first stage rather than displaying a fabricated integration screen. Finish full-screen on the presenter, then Rally and the closing line.

The emotional payoff should be recognizable: people leave knowing what to do and spend less time repeating the same conversation. Use the working product as evidence of the first step; the future graphic illustrates the ambition.

## What the larger product would earn

1. Permissioned continuity: carry a group's explicitly approved context into the next conversation, with visible scope, deletion and access controls.
2. Useful participation: facilitate decisions and clarification in the moment, with host control and traceable evidence.
3. Approved follow-through: draft next steps, ask before sending or taking action, and return to unresolved commitments in a later session.

WhatsApp connections and broader coordination are proposed development, subject to supported platform access and real integration tests. A meeting link or browser prototype alone does not establish these capabilities. Do not promise native access to every Meta surface.

## Strategic framing

Present an ambitious independent product that could make Meta's models useful in recurring group coordination. A coherent permissioned workflow, repeat use and demonstrated user benefit are more credible than an acquisition appeal. There is no evidence here that Meta intends to acquire Rally or is seeking this category. Product fit is a hypothesis to validate.

Further work should establish whether groups return, whether agreed actions gain clear owners, whether unresolved decisions get resolved, and whether users accept the permission model. Measure observed outcomes before making numerical impact claims. Real user pull would support a future strategic conversation better than adding unrelated integrations.

## Optional opening callback

Opening: “This meeting could have been an email. Somehow, it became three more meetings. I'm [Name], and I built Rally to help break that cycle.”

Use only if it feels natural in the presenter's delivery; the existing opening is also strong. The closing's “one less meeting” then resolves the opening joke. Keep the humor aimed at a familiar situation, with the working product supplying the payoff.

“Today, Rally helps teams leave with clear next steps. Next, I want to connect the conversation before the meeting with the work after it—starting with permissioned WhatsApp context, built around Meta AI. Because the best thing a meeting can produce… is one less meeting.”
