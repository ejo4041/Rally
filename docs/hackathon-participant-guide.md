## 1. Goal and problem statements

Build toward one of these problem statements:

- **Build for Your World:** Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that was not before.
- **Push the Frontier:** Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials, and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.

**The project must incorporate Meta's models.** The guide lists Muse Spark series, Muse Wonder, Muse Image, Muse Voice Transcribe, and SAM 3.1 through Meta Model API; Meta open-weight models Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open; and Muse Code coding agent for coding faster.

## 4. Rules and disqualification risks

- **Open source:** Repositories must be public.
- **Team size:** Maximum four participants. Solo participants are allowed.
- **Demo:** Highlight only the specific features, code, and functionality built during the Hackathon. Judges must clearly identify the original contributions. Failure to identify them results in immediate disqualification at judges' sole discretion.
- **New work:** Presented work must have been built during the event, clearly shown in the submission. Startups may build on existing products or codebases but must identify what was newly developed during the Hackathon.
- **Rights and policies:** Projects violating legal, ethical, or platform policies, or using code, data, or assets without the necessary rights, are disqualified.

### Sample anti-projects

The guide calls these examples generic or policy-violating and says to avoid building them. The list is not exhaustive:

- AI generating or giving medical advice, including AI Mental Health Advisor or skin blemish detector.
- Basic RAG applications.
- Simple image analyzers.
- Simple “AI for Education” chatbots.
- AI job application screener.
- AI nutrition coach.
- Personality analyzers.
- Sports analyzers or coaches.
- Anything political or involving war.

## 7. Submission

- Submit through “Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon – Cerebral Valley” by **November 4, 2026 at 9:00 PM EST**. Late submissions are not considered.
- Teams and startups submit one project, not one submission per person.
- Appoint one **Representative** to represent and act for the team/startup, including entering the project.
- Confirm the repository is publicly accessible, the video link is accessible, and everyone who worked on the project is listed on the submission page.
- Provide a **three-minute demo video** highlighting the specific features, code, and functionality built during the Hackathon.
- The video is a primary judging artifact, alongside code and the live product.

## 8. Judging

### Stage One — eligibility (pass/fail)

The idea must meet baseline viability: reasonably fit the theme and reasonably use Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models to build the project during the Hackathon.

### Stage Two — scoring

Each category is scored 0–10, with these weights:

| Category | Weight | What judges assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | 30% | Real-world potential; who benefits and how much; potential for actual use; fit with a problem statement |
| Use of Meta AI Tooling | 25% | Creative use beyond basic integration; capabilities that surprise the judges |
| Technical Depth & Execution | 20% | Sound engineering, thoughtful refinement, and craft |
| Demo | 25% | Working, impressive demo; product holds up live; compelling demo video |

### Stage Three — finals

Top five scoring projects per track advance. Final-round judges watch video demos live and rank projects using the same rubric.
